About this map & data notes ▾

What this shows. Across the six-jurisdiction study area, 8,091 of 381,852 parcels, about 1 in 47, carry an elevated heirs’ property classification, built from assessor and mail-delivery records (deceased owners still on title, long tenure, absentee ownership, vacancy, and related signals). Each census tract is shaded by the share of its parcels carrying that classification.

Rates appear only as ranges. Exact tract-level figures are not published. Class breaks (1.25% / 1.75% / 2.25% / 3.25%) are quintiles of the classified tracts, rounded to clean values.

Small tracts are protected. Tracts with fewer than 5 flagged parcels, or fewer than 100 parcels in total, are hatched and carry no rate class. That is 12 of 258 tracts, holding under 0.3% of the flagged population.

Hot & cold cores. Tracts that all six spatial-statistic combinations (each corrected for multiple testing) independently flag as significantly above or below their surroundings. There are 16 hot cores and 26 cold cores.

Zones are 11 contiguous groupings of tracts with similar heirs’ property profiles (SKATER regionalization). Zone descriptions are provisional.

Demographic layers show American Community Survey 5-year tract estimates, also in ranges. Two special-use tracts have no resident population and show no demographic class.

A floor, not a ceiling. Assessor records lag true ownership. Deeds can remain in a deceased owner’s name without appearing in any signal, so the flagged population understates the true extent of heirs’ property.

Analysis from the RHPN Phase 2 exploratory & spatial data analysis (2026). Parcel data from ReGrid assessor extracts, Q1 2026 (vintages vary by jurisdiction). Demographics from ACS 5-year estimates.
